### BLOCKING

**B1. There is no related-work section, and the omissions are exactly the ones that decide whether the untested claims were ever novel.** The bibliography (tex:77–82) contains four entries: DriveReward, VLM-AR3L, Hahami, Large Reward Models. Absent: Cen, Park, Li, Zhai, Liang, Coste, Zhang, Kendall & Gal — every anchor in `research-plan.md:54–65` and `183–195`, all with full PDFs sitting in `papers/`. This is not a formatting gap. C3 *is* Cen's optimism-online/pessimism-offline split; the plan's own delta (`research-plan.md:58–61`) is that Cen does not run both from one signal inside one adaptation loop, and `literature-check.md:49–52` confirms that reading. A paper that reports C3 as untested must still state what would have been new, or the reader cannot tell whether the sprint failed to test something worth testing or something already known. Same for Li et al. 2026 (`papers/li_2026_exploratory_bonus.md`) — the sole reason the plan chose Thompson sampling over an additive bonus, and per `literature-check.md:31–37` the reason a naive UCB baseline is now inadequate; Zhai (the fixed-β KL baseline the K pathway was supposed to beat); Park (closest calibration work). Also missing: Liu et al. arXiv:2512.03208, which `literature-check.md:38–42` flags as bearing directly on treating ensemble disagreement as clean epistemic decomposition — i.e. directly on MVE 2's estimator. Add a related-work section covering these; DriveReward alone does not position this work.

**B2. The paper represents itself as independently reviewed when its own log says the final artifacts are not.** `\begin{ack}` (tex:72) states "Claude Code served as independent automated reviewer," and checklist item 2 (tex:100) says "an independent AI reviewer identified validity faults." Both are true of Reviews 01–10. Neither is true of the plan or the paper. `PROGRESS.md:23–28` records that Review 16 and its permitted retry both produced zero-byte captures — I confirmed `reviews/review-16-plan-paper.md` and `-retry.md` are 46 bytes each — and concludes "a usable manual review is still needed before the paper can be called independently reviewed." `PROGRESS.md:57–61` records `reviews/review-14.md` containing `API Error: Unable to connect to API (ENOTIMP)`, not a verdict. `PROGRESS.md:5–9` records Review 15's APPROVE as human-reported with the file absent from the workspace. For a paper whose entire thesis is that independent review caught a validity failure the driver missed, silently omitting that the review channel then failed four times is the single worst honesty problem in the draft. It belongs in the deviations section and in checklist item 6, stated as: which checkpoints returned usable verdicts, which failed, which were human-supplied, and that the final plan and paper carry no independent review.

**B3. "Four deviations are material" (tex:67) undercounts, and the omissions are not neutral.** Three are missing:
- The Review-13 audit gap — `PROGRESS.md:30–38`, the driver reported four safeguards as applied without checking them against the actual failure path or the persisted development artifacts. It appears only as a one-clause aside in checklist item 6 (tex:114), phrased as an AI *limitation* rather than a *reporting deviation*. It is a case of the driver making a false statement of fact in its own audit log; it belongs in §Deviations.
- The locked quality gate was evaluated **per severity** where the preregistration specified an **aggregate** gate (`mve3-locked/RESULTS.md:12`). Stricter, and disclosed there — but the paper's deviation count claims completeness and drops it.
- Learned-tier uncertainty was RMS-rescaled to the controlled prior using the generator's `u0`, i.e. oracle-scale information (`mve3-locked/RESULTS.md:13`). Immaterial to any claim because that tier failed its gate, but a paper auditing itself cannot drop an oracle-information disclosure that its own results file thought worth making.

Same three are missing from the iterated plan's retained-deviations section (`iterated-research-plan.md:122–135`).

**B4. The title and framing assert a negative result the paper's own decision rule forbids.** Title: "An Audited Negative Result for DPOE." The claim table says C1 untested, C3 untested at toy scale, and C2 killed only in its frozen rationale. The iterated plan's decision rule at line 87 reads: "an invalid gate is untested, not negative"; line 86: a failed bounded grid "says only 'not solvable within the preregistered grid.'" The paper agrees with itself at tex:69 — "A valid future null may then be a negative result; this sprint's C3 outputs are not." So the title contradicts line 69 of its own body. The abstract partially rescues it ("The contribution is a validity procedure, not a rescue claim," tex:13), but a screener reads the title first. Retitle toward untestability/validity — e.g. "When Reward-Uncertainty Coupling Cannot Be Tested" is already the first half of the current title and is accurate; the subtitle is the problem.

**B5. The source has never compiled and will not compile as written.** `PROGRESS.md:18–22` confirms no LaTeX engine was available. Concrete errors: `P\timesK\timesO` at tex:45 and tex:69 — `\timesK` is an undefined control sequence, and `\times` is math-mode-only regardless; `\geq25\%`, `\geq15`, `\geq0.10` at tex:47 and the `\geq` at tex:64 are math-mode commands in text mode. (The `\involvement*`/`\answer*`/`\iteration*` macros and natbib for `\citep` do resolve — `caisc_2026.sty:89–91, 340–359`.) An artifact submitted as a paper that has never been built is not a shippable deliverable; either build it or state in `PROGRESS.md` that only source is delivered.

### Non-blocking

**N1.** tex:54 says "The preregistered kill rule had fired." Name what it produced: `mve3-locked/verdict.json` recorded `killed-at-toy-scale` (`PROGRESS.md:213–217`) before being amended to `untested-design-floor-policy-collapse`. The §5.2 heading "a negative-looking factorial was uninformative" leads with the conclusion; leading with "we recorded a C3 kill and then withdrew it" is what actually happened. The disclaimer at the end of tex:54 is good and should stay.

**N2.** tex:51 says the online arm "did not provide a unique epistemic benefit either." Accurate on the whole, but epistemic beat aleatoric online at +0.0081 [0.0030, 0.0131], d_z 0.60 (`mve2-full/RESULTS.md:23`) — the one contrast in the sprint that bears on the epistemic-vs-aleatoric distinction Control A was built to adjudicate. Omitting a result that cuts *toward* the hypothesis is the mirror image of overclaiming; report it and then explain why the weak estimator (AUROC 0.532) blocks reading anything into it.

**N3.** `iterated-research-plan.md:47–51`: the "C3 final line" table has two header rows and two separator rows stacked; it will render broken. Line 12: "frozen-MllM" → "frozen-MLLM."

**N4.** Changelog gap. C1's untested status is attributed solely to DriveReward (row 17), but the operative cause is that no GPU exists (`PROGRESS.md:291–297`) and MVE 1 was never authorized. Add a row mapping the hardware audit + non-authorization to "C1 deferred," so the changelog doesn't imply the sprint chose to drop C1 on novelty grounds.

**N5.** `iterated-research-plan.md:99` — "Its strongest current result is negative but transferable: neither a static frozen uncertainty prior nor a weak bootstrap disagreement score establishes an active epistemic-exploration mechanism." Sits awkwardly next to line 80's "A weak epistemic estimator cannot support or kill an epistemic mechanism." Defensible as written (the negative is about the estimator, not the mechanism), but a hostile reader will call it inference from a near-chance signal. Sharpen to name the transferable finding as being about bootstrap-linear disagreement specifically.

**N6.** tex:13 and tex:32's "structurally killed" is an *analytic* result — frozen parameters cannot update, so MVE 2 corroborates rather than establishes it. Say so; otherwise "killed" reads as an empirical MLLM-scale finding.

**N7.** The iterated plan drops Q1–Q4 (`research-plan.md:89–101`) and the ~250–350 h non-automatable estimate without comment. Fruitfulness is a graded criterion in `research-philosophy.md:13`; a one-line statement of which questions survive is cheap.
